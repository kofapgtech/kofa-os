A single link, no login, no invitation, no password. The client opens it and sees where their work stands.

## Creating a link

On the [Accounts](/docs/accounts) page, **Create client link** on the account. Then **Copy client link** to send it, or **Open** to see exactly what they will see — worth doing once before you send your first one.

Internal accounts have no portal link.

## What the client sees

The account name and "Read-only view", then three sections:

**Waiting for your approval** — deliverables sitting in client review. Each shows its title, project, version and due date, with **Approve** and **Request changes**. Requesting changes opens a feedback box ("What would you like changed?") and **Send feedback**. Their decision lands on the deliverable in KofaOS exactly as an internal one would, and shows in its history.

**Your projects** — each project with its status and a burn bar, and how many tasks are open.

**Recently approved** — what they have signed off, with the date.

![The client portal](/docs/img/client-portal-view.png)

## What the client does not see

This is the part worth being sure about before you send a link.

- **No money.** The burn bar shows a percentage consumed and nothing else. No budget figure, no spend, no rates, no margin.
- **No people.** No names, no hours, no timesheets, no who-did-what.
- **No other clients.** The link is scoped to one account.
- **No internal conversation.** Internal comments and review history stay internal.

They see progress and the things awaiting their decision. That is the whole surface.

> [!WARNING] A link is a key
> Anyone holding the URL can open it — that is what makes it convenient. Treat it like a shared document link: send it to the client contact, not into a group thread that outlives the engagement.

A link that no longer works shows the client: "This link isn't valid — It may have expired or been revoked. Ask your Kofa contact for a fresh one."
