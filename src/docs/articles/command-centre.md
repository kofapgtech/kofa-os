The business on one screen: "Capacity, budget health, and what is stuck — across all six departments." Where [My work](/docs/my-work) is your plate, this is everyone's.

Admins, executives and workstream leads.

## The four numbers

- **Active budget** — the total under management, with how much is consumed, the percentage, and how many projects are tracked.
- **Projects at risk** — "Over 90% of budget consumed". The number to look at first.
- **Awaiting review** — deliverables sitting in a review stage rather than moving.
- **Utilization this week** — logged hours against capacity.

![The command centre](/docs/img/command-centre-overview.png)

## Utilization

**Utilization trend** plots the last eight weeks, which is the horizon where a staffing problem is still fixable.

**Utilization by person, this week** is a bar per person, colour-coded: red over 100%, green at 70% or more, grey below. Read the reds and the greys together — one person drowning usually means another has room.

## Budget health by project

A table: **Project**, **Length**, **Burn**, **Spent**, **Budget**, **Margin**. Unbudgeted internal projects show an **Internal** chip instead of a burn bar.

Sort by burn and the top of the table is your agenda.

## Department load and stuck work

**Department load** gives each workstream its project and open-task counts, overdue count in red, and hours logged this week.

**Stuck in review** lists deliverables that have stopped moving, with their reviewer, due date and stage. Empty, it reads "Nothing waiting." — which is the goal.

> [!TIP] Read it in one order
> Projects at risk, then utilization by person, then stuck in review. Budget trouble is usually a staffing problem two weeks earlier, and stuck reviews are usually one person's inbox rather than a process failure.
